## CR • Basic Deconstruction

Q. List the 5-step process to tackle CR:
categorize, read, pre-think, eliminate, and check for sense.

A.
1) Read the question stem and identify the type.
2) Read the stimulus; find premise(s) and conclusion.
3) Focus on the conclusion; pre-think possible answers.
4) Use POE to remove wrong answers; do not force a fit.
5) Ensure the final choice makes sense in context.

## CR • Main Parts

Q. Define Premise, Conclusion, and Assumption.
Give 2 common conclusion indicators.

Premise: evidence supporting the argument. Conclusion: the claim the
author wants you to accept.
Assumption: an unstated link the argument relies on.
Conclusion indicators: thus, therefore, hence, so, consequently, it
follows that.

---

## CR • Question Families

Q. What are the three families of CR questions and two examples
in each?

A. Type I (Assume stimulus true → pick something proven):
Inference, Main Point/Must Be True.
Type II (Assume choice true → it helps):
Strengthen, Assumption (supporter/defender).
Type III (Assume choice true → it hurts): Weaken, Find the Flaw/Counter.

## CR • Common Traps

Q. Name four frequent wrong-answer patterns you should
eliminate quickly.

A. Opposite answers;
Shell game (sound-alike shift);
Out of Scope/Irrelevant;
Tone/strength mismatch.

## CR • Assumption Negation

Q. How does the Assumption Negation technique work, and when
is it most useful?

A. For necessary assumptions, negate the contender: if the negation
wrecks the argument, the original is required. Use when two–three
contenders remain.

## CR • Must Be True / Main Point

Q. To be considered for this year’s merit scholarship award, students
need perfect attendance and a 4.0 GPA. Alex is the only person in the
class who has a 4.0 but he has had 5 absences. Which conclusion is
most supported?
A) No student at this school has perfect attendance for the year
B) Some students without a 4.0 also did not have perfect attendance
C) Alex is the only student who could be considered for the award
D) No student at this school qualifies for the award this year
E) Many students have perfect attendance but never 4.0 GPAs

A. Correct: D. Both conditions are required. Alex lacks perfect
attendance; no one else has a 4.0. Therefore nobody qualifies.

## CR • Weaken

Q. A store stocks only the top 10 cell-phone brands. To increase
sales, it plans to increase inventory to the top 50. Which, if true,
points out a major flaw?
A) Top five phones have similar capabilities
B) The top 8 brands account for almost all cell phones sold
C) Users want to try lesser-known brands for added value
D) Less popular brands provide little profit to the store
E) Leading brands are losing sales to cheaper, less popular brands

A. Correct: B. If the top 8 brands account for nearly all sales, expanding
to 50 brands won’t meaningfully increase sales.

## CR • Strengthen

Q. Several companies withdrew ads from Magazine A after it
shifted from family values to sex/violence. This shows ad agencies
act from moral propriety. Which most strengthens?
A) Advertisers regularly review ad placements
B) It is rare for several advertisers to pull out simultaneously
C) Advertisers said clients would lose revenue after withdrawal
D) Advertisers placed new ads with publications emphasizing
family values
E) A reader survey shows Magazine A's content declined in quality

A. Correct: D. Moving the ads to a family-values outlet ties the
withdrawals to moral concerns, not mere strategy.

## CR • Flaw / Assumption

Q. Bacteria could have developed on Mars and arrived on Earth via
meteorite. But since no two Earth strains differ enough to be from
different planets, any Martian bacteria must have died out. The
argument is vulnerable because it fails to consider whether…
A) bacteria actually developed on Mars
B) Martian bacteria were likely transported to Earth
C) there were other means besides meteorites to bring bacteria
D) all current Earth bacteria could have arisen from transported
Martian bacteria
E) some Earth-origin strains later died out

A. Correct: D. If all current bacteria descended from transported
Martian bacteria, large protein differences would not be expected.

## CR • Resolve the Paradox

Q. Government pays farmers the amount by which market price falls short of a
target. In 1983, a severe drought caused farm-program payments to drop by $10B.
Which best explains the reduction?
A) Target price was raised before 1983
B) U.S. exported less food in 1983 than the prior year
C) Drought caused smaller harvests and higher market prices for 1983 crops
D) Farmers planned smaller crops in 1984
E) Retail food prices did not increase significantly between 1982 and 1983

A. Correct: C. Higher market prices reduce Target − Market, so
payments fall despite drought.

## CR • Reasoning / Parallel

Q. Shakespeare was the world's greatest playwright, so he wrote
the world's greatest plays; and because he wrote the greatest
plays, he is the greatest playwright. Which matches this reasoning?
A) Opinion-based comparison of poets
B) No miscarriages of justice because prisoners are guilty;
otherwise they wouldn’t be imprisoned
C) Blood test rules out disease X
D) Conflicting accusations between suspects
E) Tartar sauce example about spiciness

A. Correct: B. Both are circular: the claim proves the evidence and the
evidence proves the claim.

## CR • Assumption (Necessary)

Q. Survey: 80% of severely injured drivers/front-seat passengers were not wearing
seat belts. Conclusion: wearing seat belts greatly reduces risk of severe injury. The
conclusion is not properly drawn unless:
A) More than 20% of drivers/passengers in the survey were wearing seat belts at the time
B) Considerably more than 20% of drivers/passengers always wear seat belts in the county
C) More drivers/front-seat passengers than rear-seat passengers were severely injured
D) More than half of the drivers/front-seat passengers in the survey were not wearing seat belts at
the time
E) Most auto accidents reported do not involve any serious injury

A. Correct: B. We need a baseline showing many people usually wear
belts; otherwise the 80% figure lacks comparative force.

## RC • The Big Picture

Q. What are the four questions you should answer as you read
any passage?

A.
Why? (Main Point)
How? (Organization/structure)
What? (Key ideas of each paragraph)
What Tone? (Author’s attitude). Read general→specific; adjust
strategy, not speed.

---

## RC • Global vs Local

Q. Name three Global question types and four Local question
types you will see on GMAT RC.

Global:
Main Point/Primary Purpose; Passage Organization; Author’s
Perspective/Tone.
Local:
Specific Reference line/paragraph; Function (what a part does);
Strengthen/Weaken; Parallel Reasoning.

## RC • New vs Existing Ideas

Q. List some signal words for new vs continuing ideas.

A.
Continuations:
furthermore, for instance, for example, additionally, similarly, indeed,
in fact.
New/contrast:
however, but, nevertheless, yet, in contrast, although, still.
